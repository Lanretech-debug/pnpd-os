# VertiForge Framework

## Core Philosophy

Vertical SaaS means industry-specific software that **runs or deeply supports the core operations** of a specific vertical. It is not generic admin software.

Strong vertical SaaS:
- Embeds into daily workflows
- Solves domain-specific pain
- Reflects industry constraints and regulations
- Creates high switching costs through workflow lock-in
- Supports premium pricing and strong retention

### Horizontal vs Vertical Distinction

| | Horizontal SaaS | Vertical SaaS |
|---|---|---|
| Reach | Broad, many industries | One industry, deep |
| Example | General CRM, payments, accounting, communication | Restaurant OS, construction management, salon/fitness studio mgmt, healthcare compliance |
| Moat | Scale, brand | Domain expertise, workflow lock-in, data advantage, compliance |
| Pricing | Lower per-seat, volume-based | Premium, value-based on outcomes |
| Sales | Self-serve, product-led | Inside sales, partnerships, consultative |

## 8-Dimension Analysis Framework

When invoked, VertiForge evaluates:

### 1. Market and Timing
- Market size (TAM, SAM, SOM)
- Digitisation level of the vertical
- Infrastructure readiness (payments, mobile, cloud, AI)
- Regulatory tailwinds / headwinds
- Macro tailwinds

### 2. Pain and Workflow Depth
- Actual daily workflows in the target vertical
- Operational bottlenecks
- Where spreadsheets, WhatsApp, paper, or horizontal tools fail
- Where vertical depth creates material value

### 3. Moat and Defensibility
- Domain expertise required
- Workflow lock-in potential
- Data advantage (network effects, switching costs)
- Integration moats (ERP, accounting, payroll)
- Compliance moats
- Brand trust within vertical

### 4. Go-to-Market
- Sales motion (product-led, inside sales, field sales, partnership)
- Direct vs marketplace distribution
- Land-and-expand potential
- Onboarding and support burden
- Channel partnerships

### 5. Product and Technology
- AI-native opportunities
- Mobile-first / offline capability
- Localisation requirements
- Compliance features
- Low-code / customisation
- Integration surface area

### 6. Unit Economics and Scale
- Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)
- Lifetime Value (LTV)
- Churn / retention
- Gross margin
- Expansion revenue (upsells, modules, seats)
- Services burden
- Payback period

### 7. Competition and Whitespace
- Global players
- Local incumbents
- In-house builds (vertical builds their own)
- Workflow gaps left by horizontals
- Underserved niche segments

### 8. Risks and Adaptation
- Capital intensity
- Regulatory change exposure
- Founder-market fit
- Procurement difficulty (enterprise vs SMB)
- User training burden
- Market fragmentation
- Switching cost for users

## Anti-Cycle Role

VertiForge helps prevent agent cycles by asking:

1. What product or business milestone does this task unlock?
2. Is this governance work blocking value delivery?
3. What is the next smallest valuable customer-facing increment?
4. What evidence proves users / the business will benefit from this work?
5. Can this cleanup wait until after the next customer-facing increment?

## Business Progress Rule

Every project should have:
- **Current product phase** (P0-P7 from PNPD-OS)
- **Current business hypothesis** — what we're testing right now
- **Next valuable increment** — the smallest thing that delivers user/business value
- **Acceptance criteria** — how we know we're done
- **User/business evidence target** — what data proves the hypothesis

VertiForge helps define these when they are missing or stale.
